# Novelty: how Phase 9 compares with prior work

**Scope of the search (2026-09-28):**

- Web searches on Patoshi nonce reset, inner nonce and re-mining, nonce against time since the previous block, nonce-based miner fingerprints, and Patoshi transaction order and Merkle roots.
- Full reads of the Lerner, Lopp and satoshi-onchain documents below.
- The sources already in this repository's [SOURCES.md](../phase3/SOURCES.md) and [DISCOURSE.md](../phase4/DISCOURSE.md).

Two pages could not be retrieved: the PLOS ONE full text (scraper blocked; abstract only) and SensePost's 2017 post (snippet only). Not finding a result in these sources does not prove nobody has published it. Each claim below is bounded by this list.

| Prior work | What it established | What Phase 9 adds or changes |
|---|---|---|
| [Lerner, "The Patoshi Mining Machine"](https://bitslog.com/2020/08/22/the-patoshi-mining-machine/) (2020-08-22) | Subranges of 163,840,000 in the inner nonce. Re-mining shows decrementing, sequential scans in five parallel subranges, with nTime updated about once a minute. Ordinary nonce histograms decay; Patoshi's do not. "It seems that Patoshi was not resetting the nonce on new blocks… I couldn't find evidence of this property." | **Direct test of the open question.** Conditioning on the parent gap and using co-spent ordinary blocks as a positive control shows no restart from the subrange start in either direction, with stated power. It adds that the scan did not continue in lockstep either. His decay observation is the unconditioned form of the ordinary miners' clock and is credited as such. |
| [Lerner, "A New Mystery in Patoshi Timestamps"](https://bitslog.com/2020/06/22/a-new-mystery-in-patoshi-timestamps/) (2020-06-22) and his 2019 inversion table | About 312 s dead time; two hypotheses (sleep, or a timestamp pushed forward); Patoshi timestamps often later than the next block's. | The nonce cannot settle his dead-time question, because it carries no timing (result 3). The short-gap deficit after external blocks is explained by his known timestamp offsets, and a latency reading was tested and dropped. |
| [Lopp, "Was Satoshi a Greedy Miner?"](https://blog.lopp.net/was-satoshi-a-greedy-miner/) (2022-09-16) | Patoshi's ExtraNonce is free-running and does not seem to step on external blocks. He argues the machine slept. Hashrate 4.35 to 6 MH/s. | Consistent and independent: the nonce position is also unaffected by external blocks. His hashrate fixes the subrange scan time behind the power statement. Nothing here tests his sleep argument. |
| [satoshi-onchain EXCAVATION.md §10 and §13](https://github.com/satoshi-onchain/satoshi-onchain/blob/main/EXCAVATION.md) (2026) | Reports top-nibble-0 nonces at 11.3% (18,589 high-confidence blocks) and 26.3% (looser set) against 6.25% uniform, read as "an incremental search restarting from a low nonce each block". Bounds cores from hashrate. | **Contradicted.** On the Lopp list the share is 6.45% (6.13 to 6.78%), which is uniform. Ordinary miners are at 72.6%, so their excess matches roughly 7% and 30% contamination by ordinary blocks. It reflects the set, not Patoshi. The parent-gap test gives the same answer independently. |
| [Óskarsdóttir et al., PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0258001) (2021) | Hex-digit frequency anomalies in nonces, including an "extended Patoshi" first-nibble anomaly in early blocks (abstract level). | Unrelated mechanism (digit frequencies, no timing). Their first-nibble anomaly among early blocks is compatible with the fact that ordinary miners' nonces are concentrated low. |
| [SensePost, SHA-256 distinguisher](https://sensepost.com/blog/2017/a-distinguisher-for-sha256-using-bitcoin-mining-faster-along-the-way/) (2017; snippet) | Early client versions reset the nonce every time a block was found. | Same fact as the saved client source. Phase 9 uses it as the mechanism for the positive control and claims nothing new about it. |
| This repository, Phases 4 to 8 | Co-spending labels, 12 contradicted blocks, the post-49,973 tail, unresolved omissions, nonce band and shape, dead time. | **An independent fourth or fifth line.** The clock agrees on the 12 contradicted blocks (11 stock-like), on the tail core (0.99 non-stock), and on the clock-inconsistent unresolved blocks, where nonce shape confirms it. It adds a bound on out-of-band Patoshi blocks, which Phase 8 had listed as untestable. |
| Original client source ([0.1.0](sources/bitcoin-0.1.0_main.cpp), [v0.3.0](sources/bitcoin-v0.3.0_main.cpp)) | Transactions are gathered by passes over a txid-keyed `std::map`. | **Verification on chain.** All 321 early multi-transaction blocks, including all 22 Patoshi blocks, reproduce their Merkle root under that order. No prior on-chain check of Patoshi's transaction order was found. |

## Claims, stated at the strength the evidence supports

1. **Not found in the sources above:** a test of Lerner's "not resetting" conjecture against the parent-block gap with a positive control. The result is that Patoshi did not restart its within-subrange scan on new external blocks. The limit is a subrange scan time of 30 s or more.
2. **Not found in the sources above:** the stock-client nonce clock used as a calibrated, per-block test, conditioned on the parent gap and independent of the low-byte band. It is applied here to the contradicted, added, unresolved and band-failing tiers. The underlying fact that ordinary nonces are small is prior work (Lerner 2020, SensePost 2017).
3. **Correction of prior published work:** satoshi-onchain's "restarting from a low nonce each block" inference does not hold on the Lopp list.
4. **Not found in the sources above:** Merkle-root verification that Patoshi's multi-transaction blocks follow the stock client's transaction order.
5. **Not new:** the dead time, Patoshi timestamps running ahead, the stock client's nonce reset, and the extraNonce behaviour.
